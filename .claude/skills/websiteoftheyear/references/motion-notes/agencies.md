Legend: [O] observed in frames, [I] inferred. Timings come from ffmpeg strips at 6-12 fps, so they are accurate to roughly +/-100 ms. Many scripted "loader-wait-done" marks in the JSON were wrong: several sites were still loading when the script moved on. Where that happened, the recording shows only a loader.

Recording quality at a glance:
- Useful: locomotive, abeto (intro only), activetheory, offbrand, obys (loader + intro + scroll), malvah (gate only).
- Loader only / stalled: immersive-garden, unseen, noomo (blank pale blue + tiny logo for 17 min), aristide (counter froze at 047 for 17 min).

## Cross-site motion grammar

**What recurs [O unless marked]**
- Durations: micro-interactions 250-400 ms (scramble hover on locomotive about 300 ms, text pop-ins 200-400 ms); element reveals 500-900 ms (line masks, logo fades); hero/scene reveals 1.0-2.5 s (abeto brush 2.2 s, offbrand sphere about 0.8 s, activetheory ring about 1.5 s); loader builds 2-3 s; page covers 300-500 ms and the arrival 0.5-1.2 s. Loaders held 5-15 s here only because the pages were slow to load, not by design.
- Stagger: per-character 20-40 ms (offbrand letter pop, locomotive scramble), per-word 60-80 ms, per-line 60-100 ms, per-item in a grid 50-80 ms, loader blocks 150-250 ms. [I: durations measured to about 1 frame at 10-12 fps.]
- Easing shape: everything that is not WebGL-driven looks like an exponential/quart ease-out (fast start, long settle): cover wipes, dot pops, text slides. Scene morphs and brush masks are closer to in-out (slow-fast-slow). Scroll-linked motion is lerped (Lenis-style, factor about 0.08-0.12, 0.3-0.5 s of trailing), not eased by time. No overshoot or elastic seen anywhere, except the small dot "pop" on offbrand, which looks like a back/expo out.
- Most common reveals: (1) character-level scramble or random-order pop-in (locomotive, offbrand, malvah, aristide counters); (2) line-mask / fade stagger on text (offbrand); (3) full-screen fade from black/white (locomotive, obys); (4) shape mask (abeto brush) and pixel-mosaic (locomotive). Wipes and curtains are rarer than I expected (only unseen's rising fill, offbrand's cream panel).
- Most common hover: text scramble + underline (locomotive); permanent underline with arrow nudge (offbrand [I]); light glow on pills (activetheory [I]). There was no clear image-reveal-on-hover or magnetic effect in what I could observe.
- Cursor: the winners put the cursor into the scene, not on top of it. Offbrand's sphere dents under the pointer, activetheory's ring tilts and leaves pixel trails, malvah's cloud leans. Typical lag 0.1-0.5 s (lerp 0.08-0.15). Plain custom cursors were rare.
- Loaders: counters (immersive-garden, aristide), glyph-roll letter (unseen), thin spinner (abeto), a drawn mark (offbrand), scrambling copy (locomotive), image-flicker viewfinder (obys), radial glyph (activetheory). The strongest ones do not exit with a fade: they **turn into** the first scene (offbrand's dot becomes the sphere, obys's strip becomes the column, abeto's brush reveals the scene).
- Scroll: heavy smooth scroll everywhere there was scroll. Pinned hero with cover (locomotive), a scroll-scrubbed camera (activetheory), parallax columns (offbrand, roughly 0.85/1.0/1.2) and a fixed frame with moving content (obys) were the patterns. No velocity skew was seen. [I: possible but not distinguishable.]
- Page transitions: short and text-led (locomotive scramble, offbrand cover + letter pop). I did not see any heavy WebGL dissolve.

**What separates the best from the rest**
- One continuous actor: offbrand's sphere, activetheory's ring and obys's viewfinder remain on screen through the loader, intro and scroll, so each state change is a transformation, not a swap.
- Restraint in timing: short covers and long arrivals (about 0.3 s out, about 1 s in) feel more intentional than uniform 0.6 s everything.
- Per-character randomness: the best text motion is jittered, not uniform (random order, about 30 ms), which reads as alive.
- The weakest results here were the sites that ship a single long, unanimated hold (counters that never end, blank logos) and gave a juror nothing to see, which is also a loading risk (4 of 10 sites never left their loader).
